Shared task

Chinese document-level meaning representation parsing

Map Chinese documents into explicit, graph-based semantic structures that reach across sentence boundaries — evaluated on a Chinese Uniform Meaning Representation (UMR) data set and a document-level Chinese Abstract Meaning Representation (AMR) data set.

Chair Zhixing Xu Expected ~10–15 teams Registration TBA

The task

Participants map Chinese documents into explicit semantic structures. Both target frameworks provide graph-based representations of predicates, arguments, entities, and semantic relations, while extending analysis across sentence boundaries:

  • Chinese UMR represents entity and event coreference, temporal relations, and modal dependencies.
  • Document-level Chinese AMR provides a complementary representation of semantic connections across a document.

Evaluating on both resources benchmarks LLM-based, neural, and hybrid approaches, and tests generalization across related representation frameworks.

Why this task

The task addresses a limitation of current generative systems: although LLMs perform many language-understanding tasks well, their meaning representations remain largely implicit. For SIGHAN, it also moves Chinese semantic parsing from isolated sentences toward explicit document-level understanding — a step beyond the sentence-level Chinese AMR evaluations of recent years.

Participation

We expect approximately 10–15 participating teams, based on participation in recent Chinese semantic parsing evaluations and in previous SIGHAN shared tasks. Participating teams are invited to submit system description papers, which are reviewed and, if accepted, appear in the workshop proceedings.

Background and resources

Useful starting points for the two frameworks:

  • Julia Bonn et al. 2024. Building a Broad Infrastructure for Uniform Meaning Representations. LREC–COLING 2024 — the first release of the UMR data set.
  • Zhixing Xu, Yixuan Zhang, Bin Li, Junsheng Zhou, and Weiguang Qu. 2023. Overview of CCL23-Eval Task 2: The Third Chinese Abstract Meaning Representation Parsing Evaluation.
  • Uniform Meaning Representation project — specification, guidelines, and tooling.
  • Chinese AMR corpus — the Chinese AMR resource on GitHub.

Previous SIGHAN shared tasks

SIGHAN's shared-task tradition runs back to the International Chinese Word Segmentation Bakeoff in 2003, through named-entity recognition, spelling check, and most recently dimensional aspect-based sentiment analysis at SIGHAN-10.
