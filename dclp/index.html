A SIGHAN workshop · 2027

Diversifying Chinese Language Processing

Large language models have transformed NLP, but their benefits remain unevenly distributed across languages, varieties, domains, and historical periods. This workshop broadens the agenda of Chinese NLP by making variation itself the research problem.

Sponsor ACL SIGHAN Format One-day, hybrid Shared task Document-level meaning representation parsing
Call for papers Shared task

Motivation

Chinese NLP beyond contemporary Standard Mandarin

Chinese NLP resources and benchmarks remain concentrated on contemporary Standard Mandarin and general-domain text. Sinitic varieties, low-resource Sino-Tibetan languages, specialized domains, historical Chinese, and geographically diverse Chinese-speaking communities are substantially less represented in training data, annotated resources, and evaluation benchmarks.

Sponsored by the ACL Special Interest Group on Chinese Language Processing, this workshop brings together researchers in NLP, computational linguistics, Chinese linguistics, digital humanities, and related areas to develop resources, methods, and evaluations for underserved forms of Chinese and related languages. It also welcomes broader Chinese NLP work, with particular emphasis on reusable resources and benchmarks and on systematic evaluation of current LLMs.

The question that unifies the programme

How can Chinese NLP move beyond contemporary, general-domain Standard Mandarin and develop language technologies that generalize across linguistic varieties, specialized domains, historical periods, and geographic communities?

Scope

Four interconnected directions

These themes organise the call for papers. Work that cuts across several of them is especially welcome.

01 — Linguistic diversity

Sinitic varieties and Sino-Tibetan languages

Cantonese/Yue, Wu, Min, Hakka, Gan, and Xiang, as well as low-resource Sino-Tibetan languages: resources, modeling, transfer, speech and text processing, and LLM adaptation.

02 — Domain diversity

Specialized Chinese

Law, traditional Chinese medicine, science, finance, and other areas: domain adaptation, information extraction, retrieval-augmented methods, knowledge-intensive reasoning, and the reliability of generative AI.

03 — Temporal diversity

Classical and historical Chinese

Segmentation, syntactic and semantic analysis, historical language modeling, diachronic change, information extraction, alignment, and translation.

04 — Geographic & sociolinguistic diversity

Chinese across communities

Chinese as used across mainland China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macao, Singapore, Malaysia, and other communities: variation in vocabulary, orthography, and grammar, and code-switching.

Programme

Format

A one-day workshop with invited talks, oral presentations, posters, a shared-task session, and a panel or moderated discussion.

Remote talks are integrated into the same sessions as in-person talks. Remote presenters have live Q&A access, and prerecorded backups are collected where appropriate. Session chairs monitor both in-room and online questions, and electronic materials are made available where permitted.

The workshop requires only standard conference audiovisual facilities, reliable internet access, poster space, and the conference-supported hybrid platform.

Values

Diversity, inclusion, and research ethics

Diversity as a scientific objective

The topic itself expands academic diversity by supporting underrepresented language varieties, communities, historical periods, and domains. We seek geographic, gender, career-stage, and disciplinary diversity among organizers, reviewers, invited speakers, and panelists, and prioritize speakers who broaden representation rather than repeatedly drawing from the same senior cohort.

Outreach and access

Outreach extends beyond mainstream NLP channels to Chinese linguistics, low-resource NLP, computational historical linguistics, digital humanities, and domain-specific communities. Hybrid participation reduces geographic, financial, and visa barriers; where resources permit we will pursue mentoring and targeted support for students and early-career researchers.

Responsible data

We encourage responsible collection and release of data, including clear licensing, documentation of provenance, and protection of private or sensitive information.

Representing varieties fairly

Work on regional, historical, and community varieties is encouraged to avoid deficit-based characterizations and to document representational limitations and potential biases. Authors are expected to follow the ACL Code of Ethics.

Series

Continuing the SIGHAN workshop series

SIGHAN has played a central role in Chinese NLP through workshops and shared evaluations on segmentation, named-entity recognition, spelling correction, sentiment analysis, and semantic parsing. Ten numbered SIGHAN workshops have been held: Taipei (2002), Sapporo (2003), Barcelona (2004), Jeju Island (2005), Sydney (2006), Hyderabad (2008), Nagoya (2013), Beijing (2015), Taiwan (2017), and Bangkok (2024), alongside several CIPS–SIGHAN joint conferences.
