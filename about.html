About SIGHAN

SIGHAN is a Special Interest Group of the Association for Computational Linguistics. It provides an umbrella for researchers in industry and academia working on all aspects of Chinese language processing. The constitution was drafted in January 2001 and last updated on 25 July 2003, at the business meeting of the 2nd SIGHAN Workshop.

Statement of purpose

Under its constitution, the purpose of SIGHAN is:

  • To promote interest in all aspects of Chinese Language Processing (CLP) — including but not limited to morphological analysis and segmentation, parsing, lexicons, semantic and pragmatic analysis, machine translation, and information retrieval and extraction.
  • To promote coordination between organizations in academia and industry throughout the world performing research in CLP.
  • To provide members of the ACL with a special interest in CLP a means of exchanging news of recent research developments and other matters of interest.
  • To sponsor meetings and workshops on CLP that appear to be timely and worthwhile.

SIGHAN operates within the framework of the ACL's general guidelines for Special Interest Groups.

Membership

Membership is open to anyone who professes an interest in Chinese language processing. The official language of SIGHAN is English. The SIG does not levy membership dues. Announcements are distributed through the low-volume sighan-announce mailing list.

History

SIGHAN was established in 2001. Its constitution was modeled on that of SIGLEX. The first numbered SIGHAN workshop was held at COLING 2002 in Taipei; an earlier Chinese Language Processing workshop was held in 2000, before the SIG was formed. Ten numbered workshops have been held to date, most recently SIGHAN-10 at ACL 2024 in Bangkok, along with three CIPS–SIGHAN joint conferences on Chinese language processing.

SIGHAN is also known for its shared-task evaluations — the bakeoffs — beginning with the International Chinese Word Segmentation Bakeoff in 2003, and continuing through named-entity recognition, spelling check, sentiment analysis, and semantic parsing tasks.
